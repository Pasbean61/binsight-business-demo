The problem

Timetables empty half-empty bins and miss the full ones.

Communal bins fill at different speeds every day: short lets, students moving out, festivals, a wet weekend. A fixed round can’t see any of it. Councils pay for wasted lifts and then again for overflow clean-ups and complaints.

How it works

From bin reading to tomorrow’s rounds, every night.

BinSight sits between the sensors and the depot. Crews keep their trucks, shifts and depot. What changes is which bins they visit and in what order.

  1. Sense

    We read the fill sensors you already own, from any make. No sensors yet? We fit ours at no cost.

  2. Forecast

    Each night we forecast every bin’s fill for the next day from its own history, the weather and holidays.

  3. Plan

    Bins forecast to pass 80 % go first. Routes are built for your fleet, with tipping runs, shift limits and rain built in.

  4. Collect

    Crews follow the run sheet in the driver app. Residents report problems in theirs, and the next plan learns from it.

  5. Verify

    Each month we compare BinSight areas with matched areas still on the timetable. The gap is the saving we bill against.

      Some areas stay on the timetable. We compare cost per lift, truck-hours and overflows between BinSight areas and their matched controls, so weather, festivals and term dates cancel out.

      Questions

      What councils ask us first.

      Something else on your mind? Book a pilot call and bring your collection data. We’ll show you the plan your rounds would get.

      Do we need new sensors?

      No, if you already have fill sensors: BinSight reads any make. Edinburgh, for example, already has about 11,000 bin sensors. Where there are none, we fit ours at no cost under Savings Share and maintain them.

      How exactly is the saving calculated?

      Before go-live, we agree the cost per kilometre, per truck-hour and per overflow with your finance team. Each month we compare BinSight areas with matched control areas still on the timetable. The saving is the difference in cost per bin stop, multiplied by the stops on BinSight. Your auditors can see every number.

      What if the savings don’t appear?

      Then you pay nothing. If overflows rise above the baseline set in your pilot, we credit £60 for each one, whatever the savings.

      Will crews lose shifts?

      That is your choice. Freed hours can cut overtime and agency cover, absorb peaks like the festivals or student move-out, or be sold as Spare Shift services so the same crews earn new income for the council.

      How fast can we start?

      A pilot takes 90 days: two weeks to connect data, four weeks in shadow mode and nine weeks live. A citywide rollout usually follows ward by ward.

      Are the results on this page real?

      The bin stops, streets, wards, weather and routes are real Edinburgh data. Fill levels come from a city-scale simulation because live sensor readings are not public; every simulated figure is labelled. The pilot replaces simulation with your own measured results.
